# The loop station {#loop-station}

Audio tracks can record loops from your microphone while the beat keeps playing, like a loop pedal.

## Recording a loop

1. Add an audio track (**+ Audio** under the tracks), or start from the **Loop station** template.
2. **Arm** it with the **●** button on its row. Your browser asks for the microphone the first time; Rebeat then keeps the microphone open for the session.
3. Choose the loop length with **Loop** in the transport bar: the page length, or 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars.
4. Press **Rec** ({{key:transport.record}}).
   - While playing, recording starts on the **next bar**. The clip shows "Recording on the next bar…", then a red progress bar.
   - When stopped, playback starts first (with a count-in if **1234** is on), and recording starts with it.
5. After one loop length, the recording stops and plays back right away as a loop.

Press **Rec** again to record an **overdub layer** on top. Layers are stored separately: right-click the clip to mute a layer, **Undo last layer**, **Merge layers** into one sample, or move the **Last layer → new track**. **Double length** and **Halve length** change the loop's length. Every recording is also saved in the library under **Recordings**.

## Monitoring and latency

- While a track is armed, you hear your microphone through the track's effects. If your audio interface already lets you hear yourself, switch **Monitor while armed** off in [Settings](#settings).
- Use **headphones**, otherwise the microphone records the beat too. Without headphones, turn on **Speaker mode** (echo cancellation).
- Recordings arrive slightly late because of the sound hardware. **Calibrate…** (Settings → Audio) measures this delay by playing clicks and recording them, then shifts every recording by that amount so loops land on the beat.

## Free first loop

With **Free first loop** on (Settings → Audio) and nothing playing yet, the first recording runs until you press Rec again, and its length sets the tempo, like a loop pedal.

## Tempo changes

Loops remember the tempo they were recorded at. With **Warp** on (Sound bank, on by default), a loop follows tempo changes without changing its pitch. With Warp off, it plays at its own speed.

## Scratching

The disc button on an audio track opens a **scratch strip**: drag the platter or the strip to move the record back and forth at the speed of your hand; let go and it jumps back to where it would be in time. **Cut** silences the sound while held, for transform and crab scratches. The Performance panel has a bigger platter.
