# Sounds, notes and the piano roll {#instruments}

A [step track](#drum-machine-step-tracks) in **Notes** mode plays notes: basslines, chords, melodies. In the drum machine, each step shows its note (C2, E♭3…) or chord (Cm, A♭…), and long notes join neighboring pads.

## Sounds {#instruments-sound-sources}

A new Notes track plays a built-in synth: **Mono · Acid Bass** for bass tracks, **Poly · Warm Pad** otherwise. The drum machine's display shows what a track plays. Choose another sound by **right-clicking the track's name** (under **Sound**: Sample, Synth or Sampled instrument, with a searchable list), or in the [Inspector](panel:inspector)'s **Sound** section, which also has a sample's root note:

| Sound                  | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sample**             | Any sample, played across the keyboard. Set its **Root note**: the note it was recorded at, so every key plays the right note.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Synth**              | 91 built-in synths: 64 in the style of well-known songs and synths: basses (303 acid, Moroder, Reese, Moog, 808, wobble, TX81Z Lately, neuro…), leads (Axel F, Cars, Oxygène, chiptune, supersaw, hoover, theremin, G-funk whistle, Lucky Man…), pads (OB, CS-80, Solina, choir…), keys (Wurli, Prophet stab, music box…), plucks and stabs, and effects; 10 [wavetable](#instruments-wavetables) synths (Vowel Pad, Growl Bass, Hoover…); and 17 [physical models](#instruments-physical) (Nylon Guitar, Marimba, Tubular Bell, Bowed Cello, Flute, Clarinet… and drums for Hits: Tom, Conga, Woodblock, Cowbell, Gong). Shape them in the synth editor, or make your own with the wizard. |
| **Sampled instrument** | About 300 sampled instruments: a grand piano and electric pianos, all 128 General MIDI instruments, mallets, orchestral and folk instruments from the Versilian Community Sample Library, and a double bass, plus your own SoundFonts and multi-samples. They download the first time you use them (the status says "Loading samples…" until then) and are kept for next time.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

A synth or sampled instrument can also play **Hits**: one note per step, at the track's **hit note** (in the Inspector) plus the step's pitch, as long as the step's **Gate**. That's how you make drums with a synth: a kick with a pitch envelope, a snare of noise, a zap.

Projects from before step tracks open the way they sounded: drum tracks play hits, instrument tracks play notes, audio tracks play their clip, and a keyboard sampler is a sample playing notes.

**Transpose** moves all notes of the track up or down. The **Arpeggiator** plays the notes of a chord one after another: choose the order (Up, Down, Up/down, Random, Played), the rate, the number of octaves and the **Gate** (how long each note sounds).

## The synth wizard {#instruments-synth-wizard}

The **synth wizard** makes a new synth from a few plain questions, one at a time, no synth knowledge needed: what it's for (bass, lead, pad, keys, pluck, a sound effect), how it should be made (like a classic synth; plucked or struck, which makes a [physical model](#instruments-physical) of a string, wood, metal or a drum skin; or evolving, a [wavetable](#instruments-wavetables) whose tone keeps changing), how a note starts (at once, softly, slowly), what happens then (it holds, it fades like a piano, it's short like a pluck), its tone (warm, bright, hollow, glassy, nasal), clean or dirty, whether it moves (a slow wobble, a pulse with the beat, vibrato, a wah), how wide (one voice, a chorus, a big wall of voices), one note or chords, and anything extra (some air, an octave or a fifth on top). Later questions follow your earlier answers (a pad never asks how it ends). **▶ Try it** plays the sound so far at any step. At the end, name it: it's saved in **Your sounds**, put on the keys and opened in the synth editor's **Advanced** view, where you see every setting the wizard chose and can change it.

Open it with the wand in the Library's toolbar, from the synth editor's **⋯** menu (**New synth with the wizard…**), or the command palette (**Synth: new with the wizard**).

## The synth editor

The **synth editor** shapes the synth of a step track. Open it with **Edit synth…** in the Inspector, or right-click the track's name → **Edit sound…**. Each track that plays a synth gets its own editor tab (named after the track), so you can have several open side by side or as tabs. Double-clicking a synth in the library opens the library sound itself, without a track: you play it through the preview, a synth of yours is saved as you go, and a factory synth becomes your own copy on the first change. (There, undo is the editor's own, and MIDI learn and the scopes need a track.)

It has two views (switch at the top right; it remembers the one you used last):

- **Basic**: the synth's 8 **macros**, **Poly** / **Mono** with **Glide**, a scope of what it plays, and the keyboard. Enough to make a factory synth your own.
- **Advanced**: everything, in the blocks below.

In Advanced, the blocks come in sections that follow the path the sound takes, shown at the top (Oscillators, Filters, Envelopes, LFOs, Matrix · macros, Voice · output; click a section's title to fold it, and the editor remembers): the oscillators (with sub and noise) are mixed, then filtered, then shaped by the amp, then driven to the output. That order is fixed, as on a classic analog synth: the oscillators play side by side, and the envelopes and the LFO don't carry sound, they steer the filter, the amp or the pitch. (The order of the track's **effects** can be changed in the Inspector.)

| Section                     | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Start from**              | Load a factory synth as the starting point.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Signal flow**             | The path the sound takes, with a picture of the waveform (click it to hear a note), and a live **scope** and **spectrum** of what the synth plays. | **Source**                  | The **Oscillators**, or a [physical model](#instruments-physical): an exciter and a resonator.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Oscillators 1–3**         | Each can be on or off, **Analog** or a **[Wavetable](#instruments-wavetables)**. Analog: click a picture for its **shape**: sine, triangle, saw or pulse, or halfway between two of them (macros and the mod matrix can morph anywhere along the row; the nearest pictures light up); **Width** (pulse), **Level**, **Pan**, **Octave**, **Semi**, **Fine**; **Unison** (up to 8 stacked voices) with **Detune** and **Stereo** width; **Retrigger** (start at **Phase** on every note) or free-running; **Drift** (analog wander). Oscillators 2 and 3 can **Sync to 1** (hard sync). |
| **Sub · noise · ring · FM** | A sub oscillator (sine or square, one or two octaves down), white or pink noise, ring modulation (oscillator 1 × 2) and FM (which oscillator bends which, and how much).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Filters 1 and 2**         | **Ladder** (the warm 24 dB low-pass that self-oscillates) or **SVF** (12 dB low-, high-, band-pass or notch); filter 2 runs after filter 1 or beside it. **Cutoff**, **Reso**, **Drive** (into the filter), **Env**, **Key** and **Vel**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Envelopes**               | Amp, filter and a free mod envelope: **Attack**, **Hold**, **Decay**, **Sustain**, **Release**, their curves, **Vel** and **Loop**. | **LFOs 1–3**                | Sine, triangle, ramps, square, sample & hold or smooth random; free **Rate** or synced to the tempo (dotted and triplet too); per note or global; **Phase**, fade-in **Delay**, and 0..1 instead of −1..1. The picture shows how it moves over about two seconds.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Mod matrix**              | 8 slots: a **source** (LFOs, envelopes, velocity, note, mod wheel, aftertouch, pitch bend, random, and the macros, listed by their names: Brightness, Bite…) moves a **destination** (pitch, oscillator shape, width and level, sub, noise, ring, FM, filter cutoff and resonance, volume, pan, LFO rates) by an **amount**, optionally scaled **via** a second source.                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Voice**                   | **Poly**, **Mono** or **Legato**; glide always or only between overlapping notes; which voice to steal; **Voices**, **Glide**, **Vel curve**, **Bend** range and **Tune**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Output**                  | **Drive** (saturation), **Volume**, **Pan** and **Spread** (chords spread across stereo).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Keyboard**                | Play the synth while you shape it: held notes, softer higher up on a key. Beside it, the **pitch wheel** (springs back when you let go) and the **mod wheel** (stays where you leave it) work like a MIDI keyboard's.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

Play it on the **keyboard** at the bottom (the keys show their notes, with the octave on every C: C3, C4…): hold a key and the note sounds until you let go, so you hear the sustain and the release (click higher on a key to play softer; drag across keys for a glissando). The computer keys **A**–**L** play it too while the editor has focus (**Z** / **X** or the + / − buttons change the octave). Changes apply right away, also to a note you're holding.

The keyboard also **shows what your MIDI keyboard plays** into the synth (an Arturia MiniLab 3 or any other, the computer keys, Hold, and the play styles' strums, arpeggios and chord pads), each note lit as it sounds and softer notes lighter; when you play below or above its two octaves, it moves there. The **pitch** and **mod wheels** move with the device's wheels or touch strips. Together with the knobs' rings and dots, you see the synth react while you play the device. If you'd rather have the room, **Settings → MIDI → Synth editor keyboard → Only without a MIDI keyboard** hides the keyboard and wheels while a MIDI keyboard is connected.

Knobs that the mod matrix moves show an inner **ring**: how far the modulation can take them. While a note plays, a **dot** on the ring shows where the modulation has them right now.

Right-click any knob → **MIDI learn** to turn it from a controller, or **Automate** to move it over the song on the [timeline](#timeline-synth-automation). The switches and menus (an oscillator's On, a filter's type, an LFO's shape, the voice mode, the mod matrix's routing and amounts) have the same menu: a pad can switch a filter's type, and a lane can step it through the song. An automated control shows an **A**, and follows its lane while the song plays.

To try things out:

- **A / B** (top right) keeps two versions of the sound: B starts as a copy of A; switch to B, change things, and flip between them to compare. The track plays the side you leave it on.
- **⋯ → Init patch** starts from a plain saw; **⋯ → Randomize** moves every knob a random distance (**Amount**), in the sections you leave on (the output level and tuning never change). Undo takes it back.
- The **copy** button on an oscillator, filter, envelope or LFO copies its settings; **paste** appears on the others of its kind.
- **⋯ → Export .rbsynth…** saves the synth (with its macros and effects) as a file to share; **⋯ → Import .rbsynth…** adds one to Your sounds and puts it on the track. Dropping a `.rbsynth` file on the library imports it too.

Factory synths never change: your first edit turns the track's sound into your own copy, "Reese Bass copy" (copy 2, 3… for more), which also appears in **Your sounds** and keeps up with your edits there. The revert button takes the track back to the factory synth (the copy stays in Your sounds). **Save to library** stores the synth with the track's SOUND knobs and effects under **Your sounds** in the library; drop it on any track, in any project. Right-click any step track → **Save to Your sounds** does the same for samples and sampled instruments.

### Macros

A **macro** is one knob that moves several settings at once: **Brightness** opens the filters, **Bite** adds resonance and filter envelope, **Attack** and **Release** lengthen the envelopes, **Movement** brings in an LFO, and so on. Every synth has 8, and on a track that plays a synth they are its eight **SOUND knobs**: in the drum machine's encoders, the Inspector, the editor's Basic view and on a controller. Lock them per step (select steps, then turn a SOUND knob) to change the sound from note to note, or map them to MIDI.

Every synth starts with macros that suit it, resting where they leave its sound as it is. Some factory synths have their own: the Acid Bass's **Cutoff**, **Resonance**, **Env mod** and **Decay**, the 808 Boom's **Punch** (its pitch drop), the Numan Lead's **Sync** (the sweep of its synced oscillator), the Juno Strings' **PWM** and the Wobble Bass's **Wobble**. In **Advanced → Macros**, rename them and choose what they move: up to 4 **targets** each, with the value at 0 (**At 0**) and at 1 (**At 1**). Frequencies and times move evenly in octaves. A new target starts around the value you hear now, so adding it doesn't change the sound; and when you turn a knob that a macro moves, the macro's range follows, so the knob still works. The track keeps where its macros are; **Save to library** saves that too.

Projects from before the macros keep their sound: moved SOUND knobs on tracks that play a synth (envelope, glide, detune and the filter) were turned into edits of the track's synth when the project was first opened.

### Wavetables {#instruments-wavetables}

Each oscillator can be **Analog** (the four shapes and the morph between them) or **Wavetable**: switch it at the top of its box. A **wavetable** is a table of waveforms, each one cycle long (a **frame**), up to 256 of them. **Position** says where in the table the oscillator reads: between two frames it blends them, so moving Position changes the sound in ways a filter can't (a vowel turning into another, a pulse narrowing, harmonics coming in one by one). That movement is the wavetable sound: sweep Position with the **mod envelope** (every note sweeps), **velocity** (harder notes further), an **LFO**, a macro or a lane on the timeline.

- **The table menu** (its name, with ▾) has 16 factory tables in groups: Basic shapes, PWM and Harmonics; spectral sweeps (Odd to even, Bright to dark, a formant and a comb); Vowels and Choir; Sync sweep and a filtered saw; FM, Bitcrush and Organ; Glass & metal and Grit. Below them are **Your tables**.
- **The picture** shows the frames stacked in depth: the one in front is what you hear. **Drag it sideways to scrub** through the table (Shift for small steps, double-click to go back); click or drag the strip under it to jump. While modulation or a lane moves Position, the picture follows, and a dot on the strip shows where it is. **Hold** keeps a note sounding (the last key you played) while you scrub, so you hear it without a keyboard. Right-click the picture to **Automate** Position or to MIDI-learn it.
- **Warp** changes how the frame is read, with the **Warp** knob as its amount: **Bend** squeezes the cycle to one side (50% is straight), **Sync** and **Formant** read it faster (Formant keeps the pitch: a talking sound), **Mirror** and **Asym** fold it, **Squeeze** sharpens its middle, **Quantize** crushes it into steps, **FM** wobbles it with the next oscillator. Sync, Formant and Quantize add harsh high harmonics on purpose; the others stay clean.
- **Smooth** off jumps from frame to frame instead of blending: stepped and digital.

Tables are band-limited: high notes don't turn into noise (aliasing), and a glide doesn't jump in brightness.

**Your tables.** Make a table from a sound: select a sample in the library and choose **From the selected sample…** in the table menu (or right-click the sample → **Make a wavetable…**), import a file (**Import a file…**), or drop a WAV file on the picture. The dialog cuts it in one of three ways: **Cycles** (single cycles of its pitch, spread over the part you choose: a sung vowel or a synth sweep becomes a table that moves the way it did), **Even slices** (frames spread evenly as they are: for drums, noise, field recordings) or **Whole file** (it already is a wavetable, with frames of 2048 samples, as Serum and Vital save them). Choose **From** and **To**, how many **Frames**, and **Smooth edges** (fade each frame's ends so it doesn't click); the picture shows the frames as you go. Saved tables are in the table menu and the library's **Your sounds → Wavetables**; a project's `.rebeat` file and a synth's `.rbsynth` file take the tables they use along.

**Switching tables.** An oscillator has 8 table **slots**: slot 1 is its table, and **Add … to a new slot, then pick another** keeps the table in a slot while you pick the next. The slots show under the picture; click one to play it. To switch tables during the song (a new table at the drop), automate the oscillator's **Table** (in the knob picker or a switch's menu): its lane steps from slot to slot, named after their tables.

### Physical models {#instruments-physical}

A synth's **Source** (at the top of Advanced) can be the **Oscillators** or a **Physical model**: instead of shaping a waveform, it simulates something that makes sound. An **exciter** (what you do) sets a **resonator** (what rings) going, and a **body** colours it. Because it's played like the real thing, it answers like one: a harder note is louder and brighter, a note plucked near the end is thin, in the middle round, and every note decays by itself (set **Decay** in the resonator; the amp envelope only shapes the very start and end). Everything after it is the same as with the oscillators: filters, envelopes, LFOs, the mod matrix, macros, glide, bends and automation.

| Part          | Choices and knobs                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Exciter**   | **Pluck** (a pick or a finger), **Strike** (a mallet or a hammer), **Bow** (it sticks and slips, so a note sustains), **Blow** (breath: a reed or a jet on a tube, airy noise on the others), **Noise**, or the **Oscillators** (any synth sound rings the resonator). **Level**, **Hardness** (soft felt to a hard pick), **Position**, **Noise**, **Pressure** (bow and breath) and **Vel**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Resonator** | **String** (guitars, harps, a piano-like string), **Tube** (closed with a reed like a clarinet, or open with a jet like a flute), or the partials of a **Bar** (marimba, vibraphone, kalimba), a **Bell**, a **Membrane** (toms, congas, timpani), a **Plate** (gongs, steel) or a **Glass** (glasses, singing bowls). **Decay**, **Damping** (wood and nylon damp the highs, metal doesn't), **Stiffness** (stretched partials), **Structure** (between two kinds of the model: a tuned bar and a free one, a church bell and a handbell…), **Pickup**, **Width** (two pickups, left and right) and **Release** (how much letting go damps it: a piano's pedal or a palm mute). Bars, bells and the like have 8, 16 or 32 **partials**. |
| **Body**      | A guitar's, violin's or cello's box, a piano's soundboard, a wooden box, or a **marimba tube** that follows the note; **Size** and **Mix**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

The resonator's picture has two markers: drag the top one to move where it's excited (Position) and the bottom one to move where it's heard (Pickup); both can be automated or learned from their menus. Hold works here too. In **Basic**, the macros of a physical model are its Brightness (damping and hardness), Bite, Character (the position or the structure), Thickness (the body), Attack, Release (the decay) and Movement (the pickup on an LFO). Physical models can play **Hits** too: a membrane or a bar on a step track makes a drum that's different on every velocity.

## Entering notes

- In the grid: click a pad to add a note, then set its pitch with the **Note** encoder (Step bank) and its length with **Length**.
- From the [pad view](#pad-view): note pads in the current key, or the computer keyboard as a piano, with step entry and live recording.
- From a MIDI keyboard: notes play the selected Notes track and are recorded like pad hits.
- **Scale lock** and **chord mode** (in the pad view) keep what you play in the key.

The **key** (header bar) decides which notes are "in key" and whether notes are written with flats or sharps (E♭ in C minor, D♯ in E major).

## Recording notes {#instruments-recording-notes}

With **Rec** on and the transport playing, notes you play are written into the pattern as the playhead passes.

**Overdub or Replace** (the button beside Rec, also in the piano roll header): Overdub adds notes and keeps what's there — a note at the same pitch on the same step is replaced, but others stay. Replace clears a step's notes the first time you play onto it in this loop pass: steps you don't play over are kept, so it's a selective punch-in.

**Snap** (the magnet icon beside Q) controls how note starts and lengths land on the grid. With Snap on, they round to the **Q** grid. With Snap off, the exact timing is kept: notes land on the nearest step and the leftover sub-step distance becomes a **per-note offset**, drawn at its precise position in the piano roll and heard on playback. The grid Q (1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32) sets the snap resolution.

**Lengths** come from the time between note-on and note-off: hold a note for two steps and it records as two steps long. With Snap off, lengths are fractional. The minimum is 0.05 steps so the note is always audible.

**Chords** work naturally: hit several notes at once and all of them are recorded as a chord on the same step. Step entry while stopped has a 60 ms chord window to help with this.

**Velocity** is recorded per note from your playing strength. The step's velocity is the loudest note's.

**In the piano roll**, with Snap off or **Alt** held, dragging notes and resizing them is free (sub-step resolution). **Ctrl/Cmd+Q** quantizes the selected notes back to the Q grid.

## The piano roll

The [Piano roll](panel:piano-roll) edits the notes of the selected Notes track in the pattern you're editing. Pitches run from top (high) to bottom (low), steps from left to right; out-of-key rows are darker and the root row is highlighted.

| Do this                                              | To                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Click an empty spot (pencil tool)                    | Add a note; drag right while adding to make it longer.                               |
| Drag a note                                          | Move it in time and pitch (selected notes move together).                            |
| Drag a note's right edge                             | Change its length.                                                                   |
| Click a note / Shift+click                           | Select it / add it to the selection.                                                 |
| Drag on an empty spot with Shift (or the arrow tool) | Select notes in a box.                                                               |
| Double-click or Alt+click a note                     | Delete it.                                                                           |
| Right-click a note                                   | Its bend, slide into this note, set the length, delete. On a selected note, for all. |
| Click a key on the left                              | Hear that pitch.                                                                     |
| Drag in the **Vel** strip at the bottom              | Set velocities (of the selected notes, or of the notes under the pointer).           |

Keyboard (click in the piano roll first): Delete removes the selected notes, ↑ ↓ move them by a semitone (with Shift by an octave), ← → by a step, Ctrl/Cmd+A selects all, Ctrl/Cmd+C / V copy and paste (after the selection), Esc deselects.

**Bends** ([the Bend card](#performance-bends)): a bent note draws its bend over it, a line a semitone per row (a Fall and a Doit as a tail after the note). Right-click a note for **Bend**: a shape with the Bend card's values for it, its amount, time and delay, or **Remove the bend**; on a selection, for every selected note. The Inspector's **Bends** lists the selected steps' bends.

The toolbar sets the length of new notes, **Scale lock** (notes snap to the key while you draw or move them), and the zoom.
